Hello, I'm Kate

I'm an astrophysicist at the Astrophysics Research Centre, Queen's University Belfast. I study how magnetic activity on the Sun and other stars changes their spectra, and how to stop that activity hiding or mimicking the signals of planets.

Many exoplanets are found by measuring tiny shifts in a star's spectral lines as an orbiting planet tugs the star back and forth. Magnetic regions on the star's surface, such as dark spots and bright faculae, also shift and distort those lines. For small, Earth-like planets, the activity signal can be larger than the planet's. My work aims to measure that activity directly from the spectra so it can be separated from planetary signals.

To do this I use Sun-as-a-star observations from four instruments: HARPS-N, KPF, NEID and EXPRES. Because we can also image the Sun's surface, we can check what we see in the spectra against what is actually there. You can read more on my research page.

Background

I'm completing my PhD at Queen's University Belfast with Prof. Christopher Watson and Dr Ernst de Mooij. My thesis is on Modelling the Impact of Stellar Activity on the Detection of Exoplanets, and I expect to finish in June 2027.

From September 2025 to January 2026 I was a pre-doctoral fellow at the Flatiron Institute's Center for Computational Astrophysics in New York, working with Dr Megan Bedell and Dr Ryan Rubenzahl on new stellar activity indicators across four solar telescopes.

Before my PhD I studied Engineering Physics at Queen's University in Canada. For my undergraduate thesis with Prof. Gregg Wade, I modelled the magnetic signatures of hot stars in the ultraviolet to help set the requirements for Polstar, a proposed NASA spectropolarimetry mission.
